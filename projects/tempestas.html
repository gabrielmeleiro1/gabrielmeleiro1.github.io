Tempestas predicts how much wind power the Netherlands will produce each hour and then uses that forecast to predict the electricity price. I split it in two because the price leans so heavily on the wind, with a windy hour putting a lot of cheap power on the grid.

Forecasting the wind

The first model is a temporal convolutional network that reads the last 12 hours of weather for 23 turbine clusters (wind speed at two heights, gusts, pressure, temperature and a few more) together with recent output, and predicts hourly production for offshore and onshore wind.

This turned out harder than I expected, because the Netherlands kept building turbines between 2017 and 2025 and 2,000 MW in 2018 means something very different from 2,000 MW in 2024. So instead of raw megawatts the model predicts what share of current capacity is in use, with capacity estimated as the highest output seen over the previous year.

On the test set it's off by 110.86 MW on average, which beats every baseline I compared it with. That includes the stubborn one that just assumes the next hour will look like the current one, and plenty of models never get past it.

Forecasting the price

The second model is XGBoost, and it takes the wind forecast, recent prices and the time of day and year to predict the wholesale price in euros per megawatt hour. When I looked at which inputs it leaned on most, the offshore wind forecast came out on top, ahead of even the price an hour earlier.

Data and what's next

Weather comes from Open-Meteo, production from NED.NL and ENTSO-E, and prices from Ember. Next I'd like to add solar, which is behind a lot of the price dips these days, along with cross-border flows from the neighbouring countries.
