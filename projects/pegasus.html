Pegasus is an order book and matching engine written in C++17. It takes buy and sell orders, matches them by price and then by when they arrived, and reports every trade it makes.

How matching works

Bids and asks live in sorted maps keyed by price, and every price level keeps its orders in a queue. When a new order can trade it's matched against the best price on the other side, oldest order first, and whatever is left of a limit order joins the queue at its own price. Market orders take what's available and never rest on the book.

Each fill comes with a report showing the price, the size, which side started the trade and the ids of both orders.

Smaller details

Prices arrive as floating point numbers, which don't work well as map keys, so internally they're stored as fixed-point integers. For cancels there's a separate index from each order id straight to its spot in the queue, which saves searching the whole book. And anything that reads the book for display goes through its own methods, which can't change it.

Testing

The regression tests cover crossing orders, partial fills, market orders that run out of liquidity and snapshots that leave the book untouched. There's also an ETH/USD simulation and a benchmark harness, and each symbol can run on its own thread while keeping strict ordering inside its book.

Stop-limit orders exist in the code but get rejected for now, because the triggers aren't built yet. That's next, and after it I'd like latency percentiles in the benchmarks and time-in-force rules.
